# Peoples of Vallarith

## The common peoples

- **Humans** are everywhere, from mountain passes to desert dunes, in every shape, tongue and
  morality. They run most of the Kingdoms of Man: Cyrel, Asania, Trein, Mendle, and the human
  half of Mordun.
- **Orcs** are the oldest people, descended (they say) from the Primordial Orcs who served the
  dragons. Most live in the Badlands, and Unguruk is their great walled camp. They and humans are
  kin by every version of the origin story, which neither side enjoys hearing.
- **Halflings** are the third common people, accepted almost everywhere and settled nowhere for
  long. The joke that the first halfling was shrunk by a slighted god is old, and halflings answer
  it by being twice as stubborn. Many work the farms and breweries of Eriane, and many more the
  workshops of Wrenspire. Girione, God of Mortals, was one.
- **Dwarves** hold the north: the Dwarven Empire under the Stoneheart Clan at Dun Aldun, the
  shared city of Mordun, the lake town of Loch Torogh, and the cultists of Rulan Gaan.
- **Gnomes** are crafters, most of them in Wrenspire.

## The rare peoples

- **Elves** are closer to legend than neighbors. They keep closed societies and rarely visit
  cities. The **high elves of Brynwyr** have been at odds with humans for as long as anyone
  remembers. The **wood elves of Petrakyra** live far east, beyond the Badlands and the mountains,
  and almost nobody has been there. **Half-elves** are far more common than "true elves" and are
  treated as neither.
- **Tieflings** carry fiendish blood. In every era, frightened people treat horns as proof of
  treason. Witarr, God of the Afterlife, was one until the Ley burned the demon blood out of him.

## The monstrous peoples

- **Gnolls** of the Dramogon tribe: nomads of the northern Badlands under the shadow of Haggoth,
  the Gnoll Lord.
- **Goblins** of the Kadragar tribe: seafarers and merchants of the southern Badlands coast. Their
  god king is the Erlking.
- **Orcs, half-orcs and human exiles** of the Unguruk tribe (see above).
- **Bashrat's peoples:** frogfolk, chaos-toads, naga and lizardfolk, in the swamps and the village of
  Ota'at.

## Where humans and orcs came from

Two stories, both told in good faith:

- **The orc story.** Gor'gel killed the Dragon King, became a god, and split his new power among the
  Primordial Orcs: half got his strength and became orcs, half got his cunning and became humans.
- **The dragon story.** Ghondiir's blood and bones, spilled across the Badlands, gave rise to
  humans and orcs together.

Which is true is left open. They are not mutually exclusive.
