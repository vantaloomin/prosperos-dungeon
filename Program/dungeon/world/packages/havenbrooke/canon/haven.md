# Haven and the wider world

## Haven, New York

A small city, not a village: about 35,000 year-round residents and 5,000 students on the Haven
River in the Finger Lakes. It was founded in the 1820s as a milling town; the paper mills closed in
the seventies, and the economy now rests on the university, tourism and an artisan scene in the old
mills. Downtown is walkable and sits on the west bank, the campus on the east. Chapel Hill's
historic houses climb the east side, working neighborhoods spread west, and a bohemian strip runs
along the river where the mills were. Fae settlers came with the colonists in the 1790s, and the
Crane family of vampires has held the northern ridge since 1819. Winter is long; summer is brief
and wet.

**Downtown:** the Stag & Thistle on Main Street; Midnight Gas, the all-night diner on North Main;
Pale Press Coffee on Prescott Alley (magic-neutral); Drothe's Bookery on Bell Street; Harper's at
Main and Chapel; the Saturday Market in the central plaza.

**The Mill District,** south along the river: the Ironworks Collective's eighteen studios on Old
Foundry Row, the Velvet Spire on Riverside Warehouse Row, Arrow & Ink on Foundry Lane.

**West Haven:** student houses on Elm, Birch and Sycamore; Delta Nu Iris and Delta Omicron Theta on
Birch. Crane Hill rises above it.

**The Riverwalk:** three miles along the east bank, iced and empty in winter.

Haven PD has 42 officers, three of them esoteric liaisons. Campus incidents go to the university's
own security office unless someone is badly hurt or a non-university caller dials 911; incidents
on the ridge go to the Cranes, through their steward.

## Getting out of town

Ithaca is forty minutes southwest, with the university shuttle twice a day and the nearest bus
station. Syracuse is ninety minutes north, with Amtrak. New York is three hours by car. Seneca Lake
rentals are an hour away.
