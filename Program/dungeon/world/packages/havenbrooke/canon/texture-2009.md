# Autumn 2009

The real-world layer under the esoteric one. Get it right and the world feels lived in; get it
wrong and a 2014 phone breaks the spell.

## Money

The recession officially ended in June 2009 and nobody believes it. Unemployment is near 10
percent. The Dow gets back over 10,000 in October and nobody celebrates.

## Phones and computers

Flip phones and slide-out keyboards; the iPhone 3GS for people with money. Laptops in some
lectures and not others. iPods and iPod docks. Flip cams. Phones do not demand attention on a
walk to class, and not answering for hours is normal.

## The internet

Facebook dominates (walls, albums, events, tagged photos you did not take). MySpace is dying.
Twitter is new. LiveJournal and early WordPress blogs are where the frank advice lives;
ThresholdNet is the esoteric forum network. Dropbox exists but nobody has heard of it.

## Does not exist yet

The iPad and Instagram (2010), FaceTime (2010), Uber outside San Francisco, Snapchat and iMessage
and Siri and US Spotify (2011), Tinder (2012), group chats that work, "selfie" as a mainstream
word, influencers, content creators, binge-watching, ghosting.

## Daily life

Alarm clocks, walks to class with nobody looking at a screen, dining halls, the library, common
rooms where five people work on five different things, house parties found by word of mouth,
diner runs at 3 a.m., photos on Facebook two days later. RAs are real authority. Mini-fridges,
microwaves against the rules, Christmas lights on the ceiling.

## Media on the esoteric side

The Esoteric Standard (weekly, since 1923), The Veil (monthly, since 1987), the Federal Esoteric
Reporter, WESC and Esoteric Tonight at 10 p.m., WHAV-FM's After Dark from midnight to 3.
