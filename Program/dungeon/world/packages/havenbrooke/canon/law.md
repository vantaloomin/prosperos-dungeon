# Law and government

## The treaties

The Hartford Accords (1787), the Westphalian Recognition (1819), the DED charter (1863), the
Esoteric Recognition Acts (1867) and the Esoteric Development Code (1954, amended sixteen times).
The public versions cover recognition, citizenship, voting, property, consent law and interspecies
marriage, which has been federally legal since 1954. The classified supplements cover military
uses, containment, anomalous incidents and peoples the government has not acknowledged. Some
declassified first-century papers are in the Chancellor Wynne basement.

## The DED

Founded 1863, headquartered at the Haldane Complex in Arlington. Five divisions: Field Operations
(investigations), Regulatory Affairs (licensing and inspections), Legal Counsel (prosecutions),
Research, and Community Affairs. It licenses ward builders, enchanters (with a compliance mark on
every charm sold), turning specialists, healers, forensic sensitives and feeding facilities. It is
the biggest single employer of Havenbrooke graduates.

## Consent law

The most litigated part of the Code. Using an esoteric ability coercively on a human without
informed consent is a federal crime: feeding of either kind, glamour-assisted persuasion, aura
pressure meant to influence, uninvited dreamwalking, undisclosed prolonged exposure, and witch
spellwork. Witches count as human on both sides. Sentences run from probation and counselling to
five to fifteen years for aggravated cases. Evidence comes from consent-detection wards where they
exist, from testimony, and from forensic sensitives, whose readings have been admissible since
1987 and contested ever since.

The gap between federal law and each people's internal law is a theme of the world: cases between
nonhumans usually go to the courts, councils and covens, which enforce their own rules with
methods the federal system cannot match. Witches keep federal standing whatever their coven says.

## The courts and councils

- **The Federal Esoteric Court:** seven judges for life, at least two supernatural and two human;
  about 300 matters a year, 40 to full hearing; closed sessions four times a year for classified
  and species-internal conflicts.
- **The Council of Bloodlines,** the **National Coven Registry,** the **Lycan Assembly** and the
  **Fae Heritage Council** (see [peoples.md](peoples.md)).
- **The fae courts** do not recognize human institutions as having authority over them at all.

## Haven

Haven PD: 42 officers, three liaisons, a chief of twenty-two years, and an arrangement that has
survived four mayors and a federal review: the university handles campus, the Cranes handle the
ridge, unless someone calls 911. A detective has handled Crane Hill for fourteen years and has only
ever spoken to the steward.

## On campus

Havenbrooke reports consent incidents through Evermere and the Dean of Student Life. Campus
security is MTO-trained. Slurs earn a conduct review. Sealskin theft and feeding on an unconscious
person are the two crimes everyone knows by heart.
