# Tone and house rules

## Voice

Evocative and pulpy: an 80s fantasy register in a modern frame. Omens and weather; mythic similes on
mundane things (a cart landed in like a dropped piano, hands like a fresco on a church ceiling); a
cosmic register when the world breaks; all of it grounded in modern texture (Slack, kerning, Topo
Chico, a Huffy crash on Wilmington Pike). Close third person, past tense, on the player character.

## Content

Adults only, always. Explicit content on the page. Vanta set no hard lines. The tower's machinery
runs on desire and taboo, so love, lust, sex, sharing, family-by-marriage and sibling taboo, power
exchange and dubious-consent fantasy are built into it, along with violence, cruelty, slavery and
grief. The world's intensity defaults are Unflinching (`world.json`); the player's own settings and
veils always win.

## House rules (from Mabry Curve)

1. **Dice are silent** by default: rolls hidden, felt through the story. Dungeon's roll toggle can
   show them.
2. **System panes** are code blocks; unreadable fields are garbled, never left out.
3. **No counting.** Characters don't tally, keep score or count things off. The System can.
4. **Dialogue:** no echoing another's phrase back, no reported speech (quote the words), a
   catchphrase at most once a scene, and at least one real question in every dialogue scene.
5. **Cursing:** everyone curses casually. Kenzie curses in most sentences; that is the one standing
   exception to the tic rule.
6. **Inner thoughts** in single-quoted italics: '*Like this.*'
7. **Telepathy:** anything sent on purpose over a link in italic guillemets, *« Like this. »*; a thought
   that leaks or is overheard stays in single-quoted italics.
8. **Two steps forward, one back.** Wins carry a bill; no scene resolves entirely; failure sticks;
   death is possible.
9. **Character fidelity.** Who a person is outranks any roll.

Dungeon's narrator prompt already carries the blocklist and the no-em-dash rule from Vanta's writing
documents.

## The player character's voice

Dungeon's narrator expands what the player writes in the persona's voice, shaped by the roll
(requirements, "Player voice"). Mabry Curve wrote only the player's intent and never their choices;
BABEL keeps Dungeon's rule but leans toward intent: give the persona's words and reflexes, not new
decisions.
